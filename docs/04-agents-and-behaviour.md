## Needs (`src/needs.ts`, once per sim minute)

Residents have five needs from 0 (fine) to 1 (urgent): hunger, thirst, toileting, fatigue, social. Each rises at an hourly rate that is slower asleep; fatigue falls asleep. Meals, drinks rounds and scheduled care meet most needs (docs/05); requests cover the rest.

| Need | Hours 0→1 awake / asleep | Acted on at |
|---|---|---|
| Hunger | 6 / 40 (none for Dennis: end-of-life comfort care) | 0.85 |
| Thirst | 4 / 16 | 0.75 |
| Toileting | 3 / 6 | 0.75 (wakes a sleeper at 0.9) |
| Fatigue | 15 / falls over 7 | (drives sleep only) |
| Social | 6 / – | 0.85 |

### Tuning record (M4b, 2026-09-28)

The rates above were tuned so that a normal day is carried by the care schedule, with residents asking for help only for what the schedule doesn't cover. Target: well under 100 requests a day, mostly toileting (agreed with the user after M4a produced about 100 a day).

| Change | From | To | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thirst, awake | 3 h | 4 h | With drinks at breakfast (≈08:30), 10:30, lunch (≈12:30), 15:00, supper (≈17:45) and 20:00, the longest daytime gap is about 2.5 h. At 4 h a resident reaches the 0.75 threshold after 3 h, so rounds and meals keep ahead of thirst. At 3 h the 12:30 to 15:00 gap alone produced requests. |
| Thirst, asleep | 10 h | 16 h | Late sleepers (Arthur 22:30, Stan 22:00) woke with thirst over the threshold and asked before breakfast. Thirst builds much more slowly asleep. A warm drink at bedtime and a drink left for anyone asleep on a round cover the rest. |
| Hunger, awake | 5 h | 6 h | Breakfast to lunch is about 4 h and lunch to supper about 5 h; at 5 h residents asked for a snack before every main meal. A biscuit on the 10:30 and 15:00 rounds bridges the gap. |
| Hunger, asleep | 12 h | 40 h | Overnight hunger drove 06:30 to 08:00 snack requests from early risers. Appetite builds slowly overnight; tea and a biscuit come with morning care. |
| Hunger threshold | 0.75 | 0.85 | Residents wait for a meal that's due rather than asking; they ask only when a meal is well overdue. |
| Peggy's toileting | 2 h rate | 3 h rate + prompt every 2 h | Her prompted toileting is now *scheduled* care (a toilet prompt every 2 hours awake), as in her care plan, rather than a faster need that made her ask. The prompt keeps her below the threshold. |
| Relief amounts | thirst 0.6 | drink 0.7, meal drink 0.6, bedtime drink 0.5, sips 0.3 | Sized so one drink resets thirst for a full gap between rounds. |
| Supper snack (audit, 2026-09-29) | biscuit (hunger −0.2) at 20:00 | toast or a sandwich (−0.4) on the 20:00 round | The audit found Arthur over 0.8 hunger from about 05:30 every morning. The overnight rate is not the cause: at 40 h asleep it adds only 0.2 over 8 hours. The cause is going to bed hungry: supper at 17:30 is the last meal, and at the 6 h awake rate hunger is back near 0.7 by 22:00. A supper snack on the late drinks round (as UK homes offer) covers it; tea on waking (and toast for early risers) covers the morning. The awake and asleep rates are unchanged. |
| Dennis's hunger (audit) | 6 / 40 like everyone | none | He is on end-of-life comfort feeding and can't ask: a hunger need nobody could meet flagged him "hungry" most of the day. It stays 0; mouth care and sips at least every 120 minutes replace meals (docs/05). |

Result over a week on seeds 1 to 8: about 13 requests a day; toileting 45 to 50% (mostly Raj, who is in pads and asks), social about 25% (Arthur most; he is lonely), thirst and hunger about 13% each. Longest single wait: 21 minutes. These are tunable parameters, not measured values (docs/12).

- **Sleep** follows each resident's routine (bed to wake time, plus a 45-minute nap). A resident dozes off in bed or sitting in their chair once every need is settled; toileting at 0.9 wakes them (`resident.woke`, `resident.fell_asleep` with `where`: bed, chair or Lounge). In the Lounge they doze in their armchair (posture `dozing`) rather than going back to their room.
- **Drinks left by the bed** (someone asleep or busy on a drinks round) are drunk when they're awake and free, but only within 2 hours: after that the drink is stale, never drunk, and replaced at the next contact with staff (`drink.served` round `top_up`). Someone who needs help to drink (Raj, Dennis) never has a drink left: it's owed (`outcome: "owed"`) and given at the next contact while they're awake.
- **Company in the Lounge:** an awake resident within 3 m of another awake resident there loses social need at 1/40 a minute (double while chatting or in Bev's session).
- **Wake time** (`wokeT`): the first minute a resident is awake after their wake time, before noon. It drives tea on waking and the morning priority below.
- **Acting on a need:** the most pressing need over its threshold wins. Independent walkers (Win, Arthur, Stan) take themselves to the WC and back (`self_toilet` task). Everyone else who can ask raises a help request (`resident.requested_help` → an `assist` task). Dennis can't ask; his care is scheduled (M4b).
- Needs start near their Tuesday 06:00 values with a little seeded variation.

## Tasks and utility (`src/tasks.ts`)

- **Task kinds:** `assist` (a help request, or Peggy's scheduled toilet prompt), `care` (scheduled care: morning, bedtime, check, reposition, meal, pad change, tea, comfort (Dennis's mouth care), escort (walking Peggy or Stan to or from the Lounge)), `round` (drinks round), `self_toilet` and `self_move` (a resident's own trip to the WC or the Lounge), `lounge_check` (looking in on the Lounge), `handover`, `briefing`, `break`, `idle`. Each has a behaviour tree (`trees.ts`) and a status (open, active, paused, done).
- **Assist details** come from the care profile: toileting for someone in pads is a bedside pad change with `personal_care_staff` people (2 for Raj, with the hoist badge); for Peggy it is an escort to the WC and back (female carers only); drinks 3 min (+200 ml fluid), snacks 5, chats 10.
- **Utility matching** (each minute): every free care worker is scored against every open task: `base priority + 1.5 × minutes waiting (at most 60; + 100 × need for requests) + deadline pressure − 3 × metres away + 10 × resident's trust in them − 25 if an RN − 10 if on an interruptible break`. Base priorities: toilet prompt 90, turn and pad change 85, tea, comfort care and Lounge look-in 80, meal 75, check and drinks round 70, morning and bedtime care 60, escort 55, requests 40. The best (task, staff) match is taken repeatedly until none is left. A resident only has one thing done with them at a time.
- **Morning order** (audit): morning care and breakfast both gain up to +90 (1.5 a minute) for time since the resident woke, so washes and breakfasts go roughly in wake order and a late riser isn't left behind every breakfast. Breakfast offered first (their care is more than 30 minutes away, estimated from the care queued ahead of them and the carers available) holds their morning care until they've eaten.
- **Scarce staff:** female-only care scores +25 for women; while female-only care is waiting or still to come in the morning, the only woman on shift scores −30 for two-person work others could pair for. From 07:45 until the 08:00 medication round is done the day RN doesn't start any resident care (scheduled care, requests, drinks rounds), so the round starts on time.
- **Two-person tasks** start only when two eligible staff are at the resident; female-only tasks only go to women. Nobody holds one alone any more (the audit found carers standing 10 to 18 minutes in the corridor while other work waited). Instead, a task waiting 5 minutes by day, or a turn that's pressing (due within 25 minutes, or the floating carer has come for it), is **reserved** for its one free candidate, who keeps doing short work (tea, checks, 1-person drink, snack or chat requests, Lounge look-ins, escorts, idle activities) and takes no breaks; if the turn is pressing, its only possible partner keeps to short work too. The pair goes as soon as both are free; if the reserver is on a short task they wait a minute or two for them unless it's pressing, in which case two others go. A reservation lapses if the reserver goes off shift, starts anything longer, or is no longer allowed the task (the RN before the 08:00 round). One reservation at a time; the most pressing is reserved first, before anything else is matched. See ADR-0003.
- **Deadline pressure:** hard deadlines (checks, requests, turns, tea, Lounge look-ins) add `60 + 6 × (20 − minutes left)` in their last 20 minutes; soft ones (pad changes, comfort care) add 30 in their last 15, and an overdue soft one keeps climbing. Post-fall checks have priority 95. A bed-bound resident's morning care that falls due as a turn takes the turn on (its deadline and urgency), rather than two separate two-person visits.
- **Interruptions:** a fall pulls the nearest carer and the RN off whatever they are doing (`pullOff`): breaks and med rounds are paused and resumed later; other work goes back on the queue. While a fall is being dealt with, anyone on a day break can be called back for work (the break resumes after). A request, check or Lounge look-in within 10 minutes of its limit can call someone off a med round (turns don't: they're reserved ahead); a look-in about to go over can call someone back from a break. Someone whose shift has ended stays on for a request only they can do (e.g. female-only care when they're the last woman on shift).
- **Briefings** start when both are free at once; whoever is free first keeps to short work for up to 10 minutes rather than standing waiting; after an hour they're skipped (logged) and the written notes stand in.
- **Absorbing requests:** when scheduled care starts with a resident who has a waiting request it can meet (right kind of care, enough staff, female-only respected), the care takes it over: help counts as started, and the need is met when the care finishes. A request made *during* care already under way with the same staff is absorbed at once. A request that care due within 15 minutes will meet (open care for them, or a meal starting within 15 minutes for hunger or thirst) waits up to 15 minutes to be met on that visit (snack and morning care in one visit). A one-minute check can meet a drink or a chat, but not a two-person pad change.
- **The Lounge** (docs/05): while Peggy or Stan is there, a two-person task that would leave nobody else able to look in waits up to 10 minutes (not during a fall), and breaks are staggered so two care staff stay on the floor. A break also waits while female-only work is waiting and they're the only woman on.
- **Where staff stand:** at the bedsides if the resident is in bed, otherwise next to wherever they are (chair, floor).
- **Handovers** claim their named members as soon as they are free. **Breaks** start when due if another *carer* (not the RN) covers the floor; every break is in the staff room. The sole night carer's break waits for the floating carer's round, after its two-person work, and he's called back from it only for two-person work, anything urgent (within 10 minutes of its limit) or a fall (docs/05 "Breaks").
- **Idle behaviour** (M7, `src/idle.ts`): a care worker with nothing to do picks a weighted low-priority activity: writing care notes at reception (weight 3; the only one for nurses), tidying the room of a resident who is up (2, day only), restocking an en-suite (1), sitting with an awake, unvisited resident whose social need is at least 0.4 (8 × their need; relieves it by 1/25 a minute and counts as a check; the carer sits in a free seat within 2 m of them, such as the bedside chair when they're in bed, a neighbouring Lounge armchair or sofa seat, or a dining chair, and stands beside them if there's none; never a WC or the resident's own seat, and in the Lounge only while every resident with a Lounge routine who isn't already seated there would still have a seat), or keeping an eye on the Lounge from `Lounge.Post` while residents are there and no carer is (6). Each lasts 8 to 15 minutes (sitting 15), chosen with the `decisions` stream. Any real task, request, handover, break or fall takes the person straight off it (the activity is dropped). All of them are on the floor. The handover's floor cover, and the floating night carer while she waits on site, do a **round of checks** instead: a bedside check on whoever has been unseen longest, offering a drink if they're awake and thirst is at least 0.3. Office and reception staff stay at their workplaces (Bev's is the Lounge). Over a day about half of on-shift carer time is idle activity (mostly notes, including the quiet night); corridor standing is under 1%. Social help requests fell from 3 to 10% of requests to almost none.
- **Breaks** also wait while a two-person task is waiting, or a two-person turn is due within 45 minutes, and fewer than two others (still on shift at that time) would be free.
- **Workload** is a rolling share of the last hour spent on work (shown in the inspector).
- Every task logs `task.created`, `task.assigned`, `task.started`, `task.completed` (with `waitMins` from request to start) or `task.interrupted`.

## Behaviour trees (`src/bt.ts`)

A small hand-rolled runtime: `seq` and `sel` composites with memory (a sequence resumes at the child it was on), and leaves (`act` instant, `cond` check, `until` wait for a condition, custom leaves with their own memory). A tree definition is static TypeScript; each running task stores only plain data (child cursors, per-leaf memory, last node name), so state stays serialisable. The inspector shows the current node.

## Movement (decided, `packages/sim-engine/src/world/`)

- **Pathfinding:** hand-written A* on the 0.5 m grid (`pathfind.ts`): 8-connected, no corner-cutting, octile heuristic, ties broken by f, then h, then cell index, so paths are reproducible. Chosen over easystar.js, whose async API fights determinism.
- **Speeds:** from the persona cards (staff 1.2 m/s, Peggy 0.4, Win 0.5, Arthur 0.45, Stan 0.6; Raj and Dennis 0, moved by staff). People advance `speed × 5 s` along their path each tick; the browser interpolates.
- **Doorway wait rule:** the cells either side of each door gap form a single-occupancy zone. A person claims the zone to step into it and releases it on reaching the first cell beyond; anyone else waits at the edge (`person.waited_at_door`, emitted once per wait). A zone used during a tick stays closed until the next tick, so two people never cross the same doorway within 5 seconds. People may otherwise overlap (no local avoidance in rooms or the corridor).
- **Entering and leaving the map:** people appear at `ExitDoor` one at a time as its doorway clears (`person.arrived`), and leave from it (`person.departed`).
- **Standing spots:** everyone who stops somewhere claims that grid cell, and a person walking somewhere claims their destination cell when they set off. If a destination's cell is taken, they go to the nearest free cell in the same room (never a doorway cell). So two people never stand on the same cell; this is a per-tick invariant. The exit door isn't claimed (people pass through). Residents in bed lie on the bed cell, which nobody can walk to.
- **Standing and sitting:** arriving exactly on a point, residents sit on seats, chairs and their WC; visitors sit on seats and chairs; staff sit on staff-room and reception seats, and in the seat beside a resident they're sitting with; anywhere else they stand. Seats a carer is sitting in, or heading to, count as taken when the Lounge routine picks residents' seats. Anyone but a resident sent to a WC stands on its `.Stand` point instead (still "at" the WC for the task, like any displaced spot), and nobody is ever displaced onto a WC seat.
- **Turning points (display only):** each tick the engine notes, per person, the path corners, doorway cells, arrival points and placements they passed (`World.trail`, cleared every tick; the simulation never reads it). The server sends them as `via` so the browser can draw a turn between two updates without cutting through a wall (docs/08).
- **Room entries** are logged (`person.entered_room`) for every room a path passes through, even within one tick, and whenever someone is placed in another room without walking (a hoist, a transfer); leaving the map is `person.departed` (including conveyance to hospital). So room occupancy can be rebuilt exactly from the event log; the audit checks this every tick for everyone (the air module will rely on it).
